KAN-MLP-Mixer: A comprehensive investigation of the usage of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) for improving IMU-based Human Activity Recognition

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) have shown exceptional ability to learn complex functions but struggle with noisy real-world datasets, while multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) are tolerant to noise and efficient. A hybrid KAN-MLP model combines the strengths of both paradigms, achieving an average macro F1 score improvement of 5.33% compared to pure-MLP models across eight public HAR datasets. This model outperforms standalone KAN and MLP baselines, and integrating this strategy into other state-of-the-art architectures consistently boosts their performance. The hybrid model successfully balances precision with noise robustness and efficiency for real-world wearable sensing environments.

Interference-Aware Multi-Task Unlearning

Machine unlearning aims to remove designated training data's contribution from a model without affecting performance on remaining data. Existing work focuses on single-task settings, whereas modern models operate in multi-task setups with shared backbones. An interference-aware framework addresses this issue by combining task-aware gradient projection and instance-level gradient orthogonalization. This method achieves effective unlearning while maintaining generalization, reducing unseen interference signals (UIS) compared to strong baselines by 30.3% in full-task unlearning and 52.9% in partial-task unlearning.

DecisionBench: A Benchmark for Emergent Delegation in Long-Horizon Agentic Workflows

A benchmark substrate named DecisionBench has been introduced for emergent delegation in long-horizon agentic workflows. The substrate consists of a task suite, peer-model pool, delegation interface, skill-annotation layer, and multi-axis metric suite. Accurate Evaluation of Quickest Changepoint Detectors via Non-parametric Survival Analysis

Researchers propose non-parametric estimators for quickest changepoint detection (QCD) metrics average run length (ARL) and average detection delay (ADD), addressing limitations of ARL and ADD in real-world datasets with finite and irregular sequence lengths. Non-parametric estimators, KM-ARL and KM-ADD, are derived using a survival analysis analogy to model detection probabilities under truncation. Experiments on simulated and real-world datasets demonstrate practical utility and enhance robustness, interpretability, and empirical model selection. Python code for the estimators is available at https://github.com/TaikiMiyagawa/Kaplan-Meier-Average-Run-Length.

ReCrit: Transition-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Scientific Critic Reasoning

Large language models can fail in critic interaction by abandoning correct solutions after user criticism, which can be particularly risky in scientific reasoning. ReCrit, a transition-aware reinforcement learning framework, addresses this issue by decomposing Initial-to-Critic behavior into four quadrants: Correction, Sycophancy, Robustness, and Boundary. ReCrit rewards correction and robustness, penalizes sycophancy, and treats persistent errors as weak boundary signals. On three scientific reasoning benchmarks, ChemBench, TRQA, and EarthSE, ReCrit improves average Critic accuracy from 38.15 to 51.49 on Qwen3.5-4B and from 45.40 to 55.59 on Qwen3.5-9B.

Theory-optimal Quantization Based on Flatness

Post-training quantization has emerged as a widely adopted technique for compressing and accelerating Large Language Model (LLM) inference, but primary challenges stem from activation outliers that degrade model performance, especially at lower bit precision. Adaptive Multi-Scale Goodness Aggregation for Forward-Forward Learning

AMSGA is a novel extension of the Forward-Forward algorithm, improving stability, robustness, and generalization in local-learning neural networks by introducing multi-scale goodness aggregation across representations, adaptive curriculum-guided hard negative mining, layer-dependent adaptive thresholds, and a warm-up cosine annealing learning-rate schedule. These modifications strengthen the FF paradigm while preserving its biologically plausible and memory-efficient properties. Experiments on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST demonstrate consistent performance improvements over the baseline FF algorithm, achieving up to +1.45% improvement on MNIST and +1.50% improvement on Fashion-MNIST without significant computational overhead.

Information Processing Capacity of Stationary Physical Systems: Theory, Data-efficient Estimation Methods, and Photonic Demonstration

Physical computing systems' computational capabilities are difficult to characterize in a principled way, but researchers have extended the Information Processing Capacity (IPC) framework to stationary physical computing systems. Individual capacities are bounded by zero to one, and noise reduces this bound. Data-efficient estimation methods based on Richardson extrapolation and Sobol quasi-random sampling have been developed. A photonic computing system experimentally demonstrated higher-order nonlinear capacities induced by the Kerr effect.

A Unified Framework for Structure-Aware Clustering and Heterogeneous Causal Graph Learning

A unified framework for structure-aware clustering and heterogeneous causal graph learning is proposed, combining Directed Acyclic Graph-based Dependency Clustering via Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (DAG-DC-ADMM) with Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). The framework jointly learns cluster assignments and cluster-specific dependency structures using a smooth constraint and groupwise truncated Lasso fusion penalty. It addresses nonconvexity through the augmented Lagrangian method and solves it with an adapted version of the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) for difference-of-convex programs.

Tweedie's Formulae and Diffusion Generative Models Beyond Gaussian

Diffusion models have achieved success in generating samples from unknown data distributions by adding Gaussian noise to a target distribution and denoising score matching. Tweedie's formula has been extended to include non-Gaussian processes like geometric Brownian motion, squared Bessel, and Cox-Ingersoll-Ross processes, which were largely underexplored. This extension yields new denoising score-matching objectives for these processes. The new models have demonstrated potential in generating image and financial time series data using GBM-based diffusion models and empirical Bayes estimation under the BESQ setting.
