Interval Certifications for Multilayered Perceptrons via Lattice Traversal

A new theoretical framework reduces adversarial robustness for multilayered perceptrons to a lattice traversal problem involving input point intervals. Sound certification ensures the MLP's prediction remains unchanged within an interval $I$ containing $\mathbf{x}$, while complete certification guarantees a change in prediction upon leaving $I$. The authors introduce lattice traversal operators applied via a refine & verify iterative scheme, guaranteeing sound maximality and complete minimality using formal verifiers. They find that optimizing for complete certifications is achievable in polynomial oracle calls, unlike the strong intractability proven for sound certifications. Furthermore, logarithmic algorithms are presented for optimization within symmetric intervals, validated by an empirical evaluation using ParallelepipedoNN.

Optimal Top-$k$ Identification from Pairwise Comparisons

Addresses the active learning challenge of identifying the top-$k$ items from noisy pairwise comparisons with a fixed error probability $\delta$. The goal is to minimize the expected number of required comparisons (sample complexity). Previous research has established asymptotic optimality for many pure exploration problems, but not for top-$k$ identification via pairwise comparisons under latent utility models. Novel work characterizes the lower bound using a saddle-point formulation, leading to an efficient primal-dual procedure. This enables constructing and proving an adaptive comparison-allocation algorithm that achieves asymptotic optimality in this setting.
